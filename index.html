Job Watcher

A role I liked once had 150+ applicants by the time it showed up in my search — buried past the point any recruiter would still be reading. HR realistically reviews the first 10–20 applicants and moves on. Job Watcher exists to keep me inside that window: it watches LinkedIn continuously, and the moment a matching role goes live, I'm applying in minutes, not days — while I can still get seen.

100+
JDs scanned daily across 8 search terms — typically 3–4 alerts reach my inbox, scored 70+
Under the hood
  • JD Match (50%) — how closely the posting's language matches high-signal phrases from the roles I'm targeting
  • Resume Recency (30%) — how relevant my own experience is to that specific type of role
  • Risk Filter (20%) — catches red flags like contract terms or seniority mismatches before I waste time reading further
  • No LLM, by design — the score is rule-based and fully inspectable; I can see exactly which phrase earned or cost a role its score, and fix the model itself when it's wrong
  • Threshold: 70+ — everything below stays invisible, by design

Grocery Price Bot

Grocery Price Bot

Every quick-commerce app will tell you it's the cheapest — they're all technically lying, just not on purpose. None of them are comparing against each other, only against themselves. My wife and I hit this every week: same grocery list, three apps, three different "best price," and no honest way to know which one actually won once delivery fees kicked in. So I built the honest version — a Telegram bot you text a list like you'd text a friend ("curd, bread, paneer, shampoo 180ml"), and it checks Instamart, Flipkart Minutes, and Amazon in parallel, and tells you where to actually order from — fees, fine print, and all.

Here's what actually took the work. Price and cost aren't the same thing — a ₹300 bottle at 650ml isn't cheaper than a ₹100 bottle at 180ml, it's a different purchase you didn't ask for. A rain fee on a small basket can flip which app wins entirely. And "we couldn't check that item" is not the same claim as "we checked, it's out of stock" — conflating the two is a small lie wearing a UI icon. This bot treats all three as real decisions, not rounding errors: it reconciles mismatched pack sizes instead of comparing them at face value, prices every basket twice — dry and rain-adjusted — and only recommends splitting your order across two apps when the math survives an extra delivery fee.

Under the hood
  • Same SKU, or it doesn't count — a bigger pack at a lower price isn't a win if it's not what you asked for. Substitutes only qualify within 10% of the real price — otherwise that platform's marked out, which is what actually decides if splitting is worth it.
  • Fees decide the winner, not the sticker price — Swiggy's delivery fee shifts at ₹99 and ₹199, plus a fee you can't opt out of. Every total's computed twice — dry and rain-adjusted — because in Bangalore, that's not hypothetical.
  • A failed check isn't a "no" — an unreachable platform gets retried once, then flagged honestly — never silently reported as "not available."
  • No LLM, on purpose — the first version used one to read prices off search snippets. It hallucinated numbers. Never let a model guess what an API can just tell you.
  • Splitting is a cost, not a bonus — two orders means two delivery fees. Only recommended when the saving survives that math.
